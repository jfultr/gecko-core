**Basis:** [Mesh / App architectural model, version 0.41](../model.md) and [Gecko / Probe, version 0.9](../../2-gecko-probe/model.md). This is a temporary working list; it does not replace domain sections or imply acceptance of future decisions.

**Foundation for further work:** [Planner-Driven Build, §6.1](../model.md#61-build-planner-driven-build). The Planner calls participants directly, selects subsequent actions, and assesses the combined preparation result. Participants report results and readiness while validating particular requests. Build is a process rather than a Coordinator operation; there is no separate executable Build Plan or mandatory common Build Report. The check before Activation remains a separate contract.

**The current reconciliation of groups 6, 3, and 5 with Planner-Driven Build is complete.** Build meaning and the Planner's preparation assessment are established in §6.1. §4.2 abandons universal App serialization while retaining the Authority's atomic revision check and domain call admissibility. Dependent wording on responsibilities, Block preparation, readiness observation, recovery, and operation identity is corrected in the main document. A mandatory tree rooted at Build and Coordinator execution of preparation steps are removed. Separate contracts for Activation, overall cleanup, and actually composite operations remain; their outstanding questions are in the table.

Return to the other questions in this same plan. The identified contradictions were used as edit locations rather than a separate decision queue. This step introduced no new API formats or additional mechanisms.

**Goal:** close significant Mesh / App decisions, then review the document. Groups retain their discussion numbers; overlapping questions are considered together. All eight groups are resolved at the model level; end-to-end scenario validation and document review come next.

| 1 | Committing a decision and restarting App Authority | Established: a description revision is accepted after reliable local persistence; the acceptance operation receives `succeeded` after confirmed publication, without participant application acknowledgments. After description publication is interrupted, the creating Gecko publishes the same revision and decision without another request. Minimum records for other accepted requests, including standalone Probes, are not automatically deleted. App records are retained throughout its existence; after deletion they may be removed, old requests are rejected, and the App identifier is never reused. Detailed reports and artifacts follow a separate deletion policy; with preserved records a retry returns the former `operation.id` and explicitly unavailable report. The Planner selects calls; the system does not repair its contradictory intentions. In the first version, Coordinator participates in Activation, Stop App, and explicitly requested overall cleanup, but does not manage every Planner call. No global App lock or separate reconciliation mechanism for all concurrent calls is introduced. Concrete locks and algorithms remain in domain layers | §4.1–4.2, §6.1, §6.4–6.5, §7.3 | Resolved at the model level |
| 4 | Changing and stopping a running App | Established: preparing a new revision does not implicitly change or restart current processing. If concurrent preparation is impossible, the participant reports a stop requirement; the Planner selects further calls. Explicit Stop App is requested from the creating Gecko: Coordinator publishes a decision withdrawing execution permission and collects acknowledgments. Success requires every mandatory participant to stop; unavailable parts remain unconfirmed. Stop is not operation cancellation and does not start overall cleanup; a Block shared by other Apps is not automatically stopped. Restart requires a new explicit Activation. Domain procedures and local effects are refined in lower layers | §6.5, §7.2–7.3 | Resolved at the model level |
| 5 | Composite operation outcome under partial execution | The Planner assesses preparation without a mandatory overall Operation Report or system Build `state` (§6.1). Overall cleanup establishes `succeeded` when release of everything requested is confirmed, `failed` when a mandatory part is confirmed unsuccessful, `running` while actions or waiting continue, and `unknown` when the outcome is unresolved without confirmed failure. Incomplete cancellation preserves actual operation and part states; `cancelled` requires confirmed cessation of the entire operation and active parts, without rewriting a completed success or failure. Further cleanup is requested explicitly for the remaining scope with a new `request_id`; an old retry returns the previous operation. No automatic rollback; concrete domain details and formats belong to implementation | §6.1, §7.3, §8.2 | Resolved at the model level |

App Revision formally describes composition; the Planner selects preparation order, without a separate Build Plan. Each call has its own result; the Planner assesses combined preparation sufficiency without a mandatory Coordinator Build Report. Participants determine internal checks; a separate readiness request precedes Activation. Concrete schemas and domain call contracts are refined in lower layers | §5.1, §6.1, §6.3 | Resolved at the model level |
| 7 | Postinit scope | Established: Postinit is assessed for the selected Gecko set known to the Planner at assessment time. A new node does not reset obtained results. A blocker or unavailable participant prevents confirmation of overall Postinit for that set, but does not prohibit use of available facts and other participants. Postinit is not required for Build or Activation; the chosen composition determines information sufficiency, and current readiness is checked separately before Activation. Domain checks and formats belong to lower layers | §2.3, §6.3–6.4 | Resolved at the model level |

A View uses the same contract; connecting it does not by itself start or stop processing. Operation Report describes an operation; domain data is accessible separately. Result availability is exposed separately from Activation success; combining images and detections must identify their frame association. **Continuation order:** all eight groups are resolved at the model level. End-to-end scenario validation and document review follow. The original order 3 → 1 → 2 → 5 → 6 → 4 → 7 → 8 is retained, accounting for established decisions.

For each group, record behavior and first-version boundaries in the main document, narrow corresponding open questions, and update status in this plan. An example or proposed schema alone does not constitute an accepted decision.

**Deferred specifics:** exact types and JSON schemas of remaining fields, error codes, stage names, numeric timeouts and cache sizes; Zenoh keys, QoS, and backend; resource measurement methods, Point requirements, and Line policy reactions; notification channels and detailed Observer history formats. Behavior after losing `request_id` and retry-guarantee boundaries belong to group 2 rather than being deferred as a mere retention parameter.

**Integration tasks for closed group 1:** select and validate reliable storage, publication confirmation, and current execution permission retrieval mechanisms. They must uphold §4.2, §6.4, and §7.3; concrete implementation does not keep the group's architectural status open. Validation scenarios are listed in main document §8.6.

**Integration tasks for closed group 2:** select mechanisms for persisting and loading minimum records and snapshots, comparing requests, and protecting against concurrent retries; specify detailed report and artifact retention periods. Preserve §8.2, including the deleted-App exception; validation scenarios are in §8.6.

**Domain tasks for closed group 3:** define local concurrency, request admission, shared resource use, and concrete locks in Gecko / Probe, Line / Point / Edge, and Block / Anchor. These contracts implement §4.2 boundaries without introducing a universal App lock or Coordinator execution of preparation.

**Domain and integration tasks for closed group 5:** make cancellation formats, safe interruption points, numeric waiting periods, and local resource-release conditions concrete. Preserve common outcomes and explicit subsequent actions under §7.3 and §8.2, including no automatic rollback and no declaring incomplete cancellation complete.

**Domain and integration tasks for closed group 4:** specify permitted configuration changes, concurrent preparation conditions, local stop procedures, Stop formats, and publication of current execution permission. Preserve §6.5 boundaries: no implicit switching, confirmation of actual stopping, separate cleanup, and new Activation before restart.

**Domain and integration tasks for closed group 7:** define Gecko / Probe checks and formats for common status, assessment scope, repeated collection, and fact freshness. Preserve §2.3: partial results are immediately available; overall Postinit neither blocks preparation of the chosen composition nor replaces readiness validation.

**Domain and integration tasks for closed group 8:** define domain data formats, access and subscription methods, result availability representation, View bindings, and relationships of combined data to the processed input. Preserve §6.5: access without a mandatory screen, no implicit start when connecting a View, and distinct operation reports and domain data.

**After the eight groups:** validate the end-to-end scenario “discovery → preparation → readiness → Activation → domain result → change/failure → explicit cleanup.” Then review: remove repetition and contradictions, remove resolved questions, explicitly mark lower-layer requirements, implementation details, and future-version directions. Introduce no new mechanisms during review without separate discussion.

**Plan completion condition:** all eight groups have agreed rules or explicit exclusion from first-version scope; the end-to-end scenario is validated; the main text and open questions are consistent. The temporary plan can then be deleted in a separate change.
